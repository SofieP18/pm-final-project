Slide 5 · Strategy

Consumers struggle to find and agree on affordable repayment plans

Problem hook

Consumers struggle to find and agree on affordable repayment plans, leading to abandoned journeys, missed recovery opportunities, higher operational costs and unnecessary agent contact.


Strategic crisis

It risks losing competitiveness as other companies offer smoother digital repayment solutions, while operational costs remain high and recovery opportunities are missed.

Value proposition

Create affordable instalment plans that customers can understand and keep

For a consumer with one or two open claims who works irregular shifts and has no fixed monthly income, we will provide a simple, transparent and personalised digital journey because we want customers to set up affordable instalment plans, while helping Riverty improve debt recovery, increase self-service and reduce manual work for collection agents.

Data-backed hypothesis · Optimizing the existing

Based on that agreements with missed payment during 60 days is 19%, we believe that solving the income and plan commitments questions at the moment of decision for a consumer with one or two open claims who works irregular shifts and has no fixed monthly income will result in finishing the income step in one sitting, and in her choosing a plan by its terms and consequences instead of by screen order or length. She also keeps the first payments, because she knew what she was committing to. It maps to Retention, Churn and Revenue, as measured by when the reached income document step gets over 83.7%. We will protect the 60-day missed-payment rate among shift-income agreements (baseline 22.5%, versus 17.4% for fixed salary). Decision window: a 50/50 test on shift-income starters, read once, 10 weeks after enrolment stops (week 36 at the latest). Ship if reach improves by at least 3 points and the 60-day missed-payment rate stays within the guardrail; otherwise iterate or kill.

Slide 6 · Research

The workaround she uses today is the status quo to beat

Competitive analysis / workaround
  • Persona: a consumer with one or two open claims who works irregular shifts and has no fixed monthly income.
  • External tools: uses the “plan order” as a decision rule.
  • Process: gets past the affordability step despite the ambiguity, lacks a way to test what she can safely afford, so she substitutes a shortcut: the fewest-months option looks like the “right answer”. Commits, finds the amount too high and misses the second payment.
  • Evidence: “I picked the plan with the lowest number of months because it looked like the right answer. The amount was too high and I missed the second payment”
Competitive advantages
  • Fewer drop-offs: plain-language income prompts and a “safe amount” default replace call-back guesswork.
  • No repeated checks for agents: entered data, confidence flags and reasons arrive with the case, so the agent corrects exceptions instead of re-asking everything.
  • Plans built to be kept: affordability-bounded options reduce missed payments, so a rising SSR reflects better outcomes rather than plans that collapse.

Slide 8 · Validation

Experiment plan: Income explainer, 50/50, one read at week 36 at the latest

Hypothesis

An Income explainer for a consumer with one or two open claims who works irregular shifts will result in her reaching the income document step in the same session in which she reaches the income question, because she knows which income to enter and for which period. We will protect the 60-day missed-payment rate among shift-income agreements (baseline 22.5%) throughout the test.


Control (A) vs Variant (B)
  • A: no definition and no stated period at the income step; she can’t tell whether “income” means this month or a normal month.
  • B: an inline explainer at the income field stating which income to enter, for which period, and what to do when pay varies.
  • Held constant: same build, audience, screens, field rules, agent scripts and logging; B5 frozen for the whole window.

Slide 9 · Launch

Slide 10 · Story

Friction points, the aha moment and what comes next

Friction points & the aha moment

What was hard

Taking in everything AI produced. It generated a lot of information and data very quickly, and with the limited time we had, it was hard to read, check and decide what actually mattered.


What surprised me

AI smoothed over a real frustration, labelling “reminder sent after the due date” as minor technical debt.


The aha moment

When I understood the “what is the exact moment she gives up” and could base the concept about that.

Key takeaways / what I would do next

Biggest learnings

  • AI executes, I decide what is worth asking and whether the answer is right.
  • One sharp moment of misery beats a long feature list.
  • A metric win only counts if guardrails hold.

Next

Build my AI skills further, so I can prompt better, check outputs faster and get more useful help and understanding from AI in future projects.
