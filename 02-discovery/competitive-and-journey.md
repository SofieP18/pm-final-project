# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** A consumer with one or two open claims who works irregular shifts and has no fixed monthly income.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Set up a repayment plan that fits what she can actually pay, without risking a missed payment.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** On her break, the portal asks for her income and she cannot tell whether it means this month or a normal month. She closes the journey intending to return. In the version of her story where she does continue, she picks the plan with the fewest months because it looks like the “right answer,” finds the amount too high, and misses the second payment.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Use the "plan order" as a decision rule
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** Gets past the affordability step despite the ambiguity.
Lacks a way to test what she can safely afford, so she substitutes a shortcut: the fewest-months option looks like the “right answer.”
Commits to that plan.
Finds the amount too high and misses the second payment.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** The plan review screen, at the instant she chooses the plan
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** “I picked the plan with the lowest number of months because it looked like the right answer. The amount was too high and I missed the second payment.”
