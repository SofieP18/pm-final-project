# Hypothesis & Success Metrics (Module 3)

## Pre-work · Hypothesis check
- **Role , who you are solving for (from M2):** A consumer with one or two open claims who works irregular shifts and has no fixed monthly income
- **Goal , what this user is ultimately trying to achieve:** Set up a repayment plan that fits what she can actually pay, without risking a missed payment
- **Friction / moment of misery , the specific pain blocking their goal:** On her break, the portal asks for her income and she cannot tell whether it means this month or a normal month. She closes the journey intending to return. In the version of her story where she does continue, she picks the plan with the fewest months because it looks like the “right answer,” finds the amount too high, and misses the second payment
- **Current workaround , the external tool or manual process they rely on (M2):** Use the "plan order" as a decision rule
- **Problem Hook , your one-sentence framing of the business crisis (M1):** Consumers struggle to find and agree on affordable repayment plans, leading to abandoned journeys, missed recovery opportunities, higher operational costs and unnecessary agent contact
- **Value Proposition , the outcome your initiative promised to deliver (M1):** For a consumer with one or two open claims who works irregular shifts and has no fixed monthly income, we will provide a simple, transparent and personalised digital journey because we want customers to set up affordable instalment plans, while helping Riverty improve debt recovery, increase self-service and reduce manual work for collection agents

## Read the evidence in your case
- **Where does your case say people stall or leave? Does that match the friction you wrote in Module 2?:** Inside the instalment journey
- **What got better or worse in the case, and does that support the workaround your persona uses?:** Better: Agreement rate rose from 43.1% to 46.0%.
Agent contact within 7 days fell from 33.4% to 31.0%.
Self-service resolution rose from 31.1% to 34.7%.
Worse:
60-day missed payments rose from 15.2% to 19.0%.

The pattern fits. When the screen’s first option became the shorter plan, more people agreed and more then missed payments.
- **Which user in the case is carrying the pain, and is that the person your Module 2 persona describes?:** Yes, the multi-claim consumers carry the conversion and agent-load pain and shift workers carry the broken-plan pain.
